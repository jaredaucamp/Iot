Chapter One — The Problem

Shared buildings share something else: invisible losses.

Complexes, estates, blocks of flats and shopping centres all have the same blind spot — nobody can see usage per unit, so nobody can fix what's actually wrong.

The leak nobody bills for

A slow leak behind a wall or under a slab can run for months before anyone notices. By then it's the body corporate footing the bill — not the unit that used it.

One meter, twenty owners

Bulk electricity split evenly across units means the resident who runs air-con all day pays the same as the one who's never home. Someone always loses that argument.

Disputes that never end

Without individual data, every high bill becomes an AGM argument. Smart metering turns "I think you used more" into a number everyone in the room can see.

Two floors below still run on old, dumb meters — one of them is leaking right now. The ground floor already has ours installed.

Chapter Two — The Shift

What if every unit reported back, on its own?

A smart meter doesn't just measure — it reports, in real time, over a secure connection. That's how a hidden number becomes one everyone can trust.
